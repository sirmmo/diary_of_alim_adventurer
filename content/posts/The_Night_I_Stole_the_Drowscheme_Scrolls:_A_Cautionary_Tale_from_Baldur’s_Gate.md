*By Alim Kor, Retired Adventurer & Council Advisor to the Guild of Emerald Enclaves, Silverymoon*  

### Prologue: The City of Blood and Shadows  

Let me make one thing clear: I *loathe* Baldur’s Gate.  

It’s not the grime clinging to its cobblestones, the ever-present stench of stale ale and brine, or even the cutthroats lurking in its sewers—though those things hardly endear it to a man of taste. No, it’s the *vibe*. The city breathes treachery. Every transaction hides a double-cross, every smile a knife. And in my line of work, professional paranoia isn’t just prudent—it’s survival.  

Which is precisely why, twelve summers past, I found myself waist-deep in its filth.  

The job came through Mistress Vaelis, a Moon Elf diplomat who frequented Silverymoon’s Court of Stars. She claimed the Black Dagger trading syndicate—a Guild-linked outfit with fingers in *every* pie from Luskan to Amn—had intercepted whispers of a plot involving "surface-dwelling allies of the Spider Queen." The specifics? A set of encrypted maps and tactical scrolls detailing a coordinated drow assault on Baldur’s Gate’s Outer City slums—a feint to collapse the Flaming Fist garrison while their true target, the High Hall’s magically warded vaults, was plundered.  

Why me? Officially, my "flexible morals" and experience with Underdark incursions. Unofficially? Vaelis knew I owed the Black Daggers for a botched Calimshan relic job. Debt makes for powerful motivation.  

### Act I: The Elfsong’s Discordant Melody  

Baldur’s Gate in late autumn is a study in damp misery. Rain sleets sideways off the Chionthar, turning the Lower City into a cesspool of mud and rotting fish guts. My contact point? The Elfsong Tavern—because of *course* it was.  

I’d arranged to meet "Gellert," a tiefling information broker whose horns had been filed down to nubs (a fashion statement or punishment? I never asked). We occupied a smoke-hazed corner booth while a tone-deaf bard butchered Ballads of the Weave.  

"The drow aren’t working alone," Gellert hissed, sliding a charcoal sketch across the table: a spired manor in the Upper City, draped in violet Bhaalist pennants. "House Zauvirr’s got a surface annex here. Noble facade. Staffed by *Szarkai*."  

My stomach tightened. *Szarkai*—"moon whites"—were albino drow spies, magically altered to pass as surface elves. Perfect infiltrators.  

"Scrolls are in their vault," Gellert continued. "Key’s split. First half’s on the Steward, a *Szarkai* named Naeloth. Second’s carried by the House Matron herself, Zovarra. They meet nightly in the sanctum to update the ciphers."  

Stealing both key fragments undetected? Impossible. Which meant alternative entry.  

"The vault’s on the third sublevel," I noted, studying the sketch. "Any structural weaknesses?"  

Gellert’s grin revealed filed teeth. "Old sewer tunnels beneath the manor. Collapsed during the Damnation Siege. Rumored to connect to their wine cellar. Also rumored to house a Gelugon."  

Of course. A *Gelugon*—an ice devil. Because why make things easy?  

### Act II: Wine, Devils, and Poor Life Choices  

Three nights later, I crouched in knee-deep sewage, flintlock in one hand, a pilfered Streetcleaner’s Guild badge glowing faintly in the other. The tunnel stank of ammonia and despair.  

My crew? A temporary alliance of desperation:  

- **Kargas**, a one-eyed half-orc sapper with a love for blasting powder and terrible poetry.  
- **Lysara**, a former Fist marshal turned grave robber, whose lockpicks were rumored to be made from a mimic’s bones.  
- And **Marrow**, my least favorite sentient companion: a **Dustbone Skeleton** bound to a brass knucklebone I’d won in a bet with a necromancer. It communicated via aggressive charades.  

The plan was simple (a red flag, in hindsight):  

1. **Kargas** blows a "subtle" hole in the sewer wall beneath the manor’s cellar.  
2. **Lysara** circumvents the arcane wards on the service stairs.  
3. **Marrow** distracts the Gelugon (somehow).  
4. I sneak into the sanctum during Zovarra and Naeloth’s meeting, nab both key halves, and descend to the vault.  

Everything went wrong by minute two.  

Kargas’s "controlled blast" vaporized six feet of wall… and collapsed the western cellar shelf, flooding the tunnel with 80-year-old Chessentan merlot. Lysara, soaked in wine, triggered a glyph alarm while picking the stairwell lock. Shouting erupted above. Marrow, inexplicably, hurled its own tibia at the Gelugon’s head.  

The devil turned, exhaling a cone of frost that flash-froze the wine puddles—and Kargas’s left boot.  

"New plan!" I yelled, firing my flintlock at the Gelugon’s chain-clad thigh. The bullet ricocheted into a cask of Arborian pinot noir. "Lysara—get the vault open! Kargas—improvise!"  

What followed was less "covert heist" and more "drunken bar brawl." Kargas wielded a flaming rum cask like a mace. Lysara jammed her mimic-bone pick into the Gelugon’s knee joint, freezing her arm solid in retaliation. Marrow, now hip-deep in thawing merlot, fashioned a sling from its own rib cage to pelt the devil with pickled radishes.  

I scrambled upstairs.  

### Act III: Dancing with Shadows  

The sanctum was worse.  

Zovarra—Matron of House Zauvirr—stood flanked by two drider sentinels, mandibles clicking rhythmically. Naeloth, her steward, held a scroll case carved from Underdark crystal. They weren’t arguing.  

They were *flirting*.  

"—insolence cannot stand, *phuut dos*," Zovarra purred in accented Common, trailing a claw down Naeloth’s jaw. "When Lloth’s victory bleeds the surface white, I’ll claim the Dukal seat. And you, *my sweet abbil*, shall savor the spoils."  

Naeloth smirked. "If the *Khalreshaar* mercenaries reach the Gate before the Moonrise…"  

*Khalreshaar*. I stiffened. That wasn’t a drow house—it was a shadow-elf mercenary company from the Frozenfar. The scope widened: this wasn’t just a raid. It was an *alliance*.  

The scroll case glinted. Both key halves hung around their necks on adamantine chains. Crude violence wouldn’t work; drow poison glands can put down a troll in seconds.  

So I cheated.  

Years ago, I’d lifted a **Stone of Ill Luck** from a Thayan crypt. It curses the wielder with comical misfortune—but when thrown into an enclosed space? Pure chaos.  

The stone arced into the sanctum, clattering against a Braeryn-made teapot.  

***Clang-CRASH!***  

Zovarra’s claw snagged Naeloth’s ceremonial collar, tearing it. Naeloth stumbled, knocking over a brazier. Embers ignited a tapestry depicting Lolth’s ascension. A drider shrieked, flailing into a bookshelf. Ritual components—bat guano, sulphur, a *very* fragile drow egg sac—rained down.  

Amidst the smoke and spiderling panic, I lunged.  

The keys came free with two swift tugs. Zovarra’s envenomed dagger grazed my shoulder—numbness bloomed like ice—but I was already moving, half-tumbling down the service stairwell.  

"Vault!" I choked out to Lysara. "Seal it behind me!"  

### Act IV: Lies, Ink, and Consequences  

The vault was disappointingly mundane: lead-lined walls, a single pedestal holding the **Drowscheme Scrolls** inside a glass bell jar. No traps. No guardians.  

Just seven parchments covered in graceful, looping Espruar script.  

I cracked the seal. And froze.  

The tactical maps showed assault vectors, yes—but the targets weren’t just the Outer City. They included safehouses for Harper agents, Emerald Enclave caches, even the Graycloak barracks. Worse, the mercenary pact wasn’t with shadow-elves…  

It was with **Dagult Neverember**.  

The ex-Open Lord of Waterdeep, exiled for embezzlement, had promised the drow funding and surface legitimacy in exchange for their help reclaiming his seat. Rigged elections. Manufactured riots. Assassinations. The scrolls laid it all out—including Neverember’s payment ledgers.  

This was bigger than Baldur’s Gate. Political. Cataclysmic.  

And utterly beyond my pay grade.  

Footsteps pounded above—Zovarra’s shrieks, drider skittering. I could’ve burned the scrolls. Contained the nightmare.  

Instead, I stole them.  

Why? Greed? Stupidity? The thrill?  

No. Because secrets have value. And leverage keeps you alive in places like Baldur’s Gate.  

### Epilogue: Bitter Aftermath  

Lysara sealed the vault with Kargas’s last smoke pellet. Marrow carried my poison-slowed carcass back through the sewers. We lost the Gelugon when it paused to admire its frozen reflection.  

The scrolls reached Mistress Vaelis. Neverember’s plots were thwarted (temporarily). House Zauvirr’s Baldur’s Gate cell was purged by Harpers—though Zovarra escaped.  

My payment? A Black Dagger pardon, two healing potions, and a scar that still aches when it rains.  

But here’s what nobody tells you about "saving the day":  

The drow retaliated.  

A tenday later, Flaming Fist found Gellert’s body in the harbor, his lungs stuffed with live spiders. Kargas vanished—likely devoured by the Gelugon, who now reportedly runs a smugglers’ ring in the Undercellar. Lysara quit grave robbing, joined a Selûnite cloister, and won’t speak to me.  

As for the scrolls? Neverember’s still out there. The Zhentarim have the ledgers now—or so rumor claims. And Silverymoon’s Guilds still debate whether burning the parchments would’ve been wiser than leveraging them.  

I don’t regret the theft. Regret implies you’d change things.  

But I understand the cost.  

These days, I counsel younger adventurers at the Emerald Enclave. When they ask about Baldur’s Gate, I tell them this:  

The darkest shadows aren’t in dungeons. They’re in the deals we make to survive them.  

-A.K.
