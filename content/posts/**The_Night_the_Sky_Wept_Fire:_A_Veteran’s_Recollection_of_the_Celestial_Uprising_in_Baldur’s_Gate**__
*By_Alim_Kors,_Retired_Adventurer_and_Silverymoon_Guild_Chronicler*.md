### Prologue: The Quiet Before the Storm  
I’ve seen cities burn, dungeons swallow heroes whole, and dragons blot out the sun. Yet few memories cling to my bones like the night Baldur’s Gate became a stage for the heavens themselves—a night when faith, folly, and the fury of nature collided. It was a decade ago, but I recount it now not to revel in chaos, but to remind us all: even in the heart of civilization, the veil between our world and the sublime is thinner than we dare admit.  

I was in Baldur’s Gate on behalf of the Silver Marches Traders’ Consortium—a tedious task of negotiating tariffs that made me yearn for the days of sparring with orcs. What unfurled instead was a spectacle that reshaped my understanding of mortal ambition.  

---

### Chapter I: Shadows Beneath the Torchlit Streets  
Baldur’s Gate in those days was a creature of contrasts. Refugees from the Cloakwood huddled in the Outer City, while nobles in the Upper City sipped wine steeped in obliviousness. Yet an undercurrent thrummed beneath the usual squalor. Rumors spread of strange symbols etched onto the dockside warehouses—glowing sigils that vanished by dawn. Fishermen whispered of lights dancing beneath the waves of the Chionthar. The Flaming Fist dismissed it as superstition. I should’ve known better.  

On the third evening, I found myself at the Dragon’s Postern, nursing an ale thick enough to chew, when a Harper agent named Elara seized my arm. Her cloak reeked of brine and desperation. “They’re gathering tonight,” she hissed. “Near the Stormshore Tabernacle. They mean to tear something *open*.”  

“Cultists?” I asked, already buckling my scabbard.  

“Worse. They call themselves the ‘Choir of the Unbound Sky.’ They seek to merge Faerûn with the ‘True Heavens’—whatever madness that entails.”  

---

### Chapter II: The Choir’s Symphony of Madness  
We crept through the Lower City’s labyrinth, where the cultists had painted the streets with iridescent dust that hummed beneath our boots. The Tabernacle loomed ahead, its spires clawing at a cloudless night sky—but the moon was wrong. Selûne’s Tears, usually a scatter of faint stars, blazed like freshly kindled embers, their light tinged vermillion.  

The cultists stood in a helix pattern, fifty strong, robed in fabric that mirrored the night sky. At their center hovered a relic: a jagged shard of obsidian pulsing with trapped lightning. I recognized it—a *sky-keel*, a fragment from a long-crashed spelljammer ship. Such artifacts channel celestial energy… recklessly.  

Their leader, a gaunt elf with eyes like smoked glass, raised the shard and began a chant in Primordial. The air vibrated. Stones levitated. Elara muttered, “They’re using the shard as a conduit. They mean to drag a piece of the Astral Plane into the Material.”  

“Why here?” I whispered.  

“The Tabernacle’s latent divine energy,” she said. “A beacon.”  

Before we could act, the Flaming Fist arrived—not with stealth, but with battering rams and crossbows. Captain Borlus, a bull-necked man who favored force over finesse, bellowed, “Disperse, filth!”  

Chaos erupted.  

---

### *Interlude: On the Fragility of Mortal Plans*  
Adventurers learn early: no stratagem survives first contact. The cultists didn’t flee; they *laughed*. Their chant grew louder, and the sky-keel flared. Borlus’s arrows vaporized mid-flight. That’s when the true phenomenon began.  

---

### Chapter III: The Heavens Align—And Weep  
The temperature plummeted. Frost spiderwebbed across the Tabernacle’s stones. Above, the Tears of Selûne *moved*, swirling into a vortex that bathed the city in spectral light. From the rift, ribbons of liquid fire poured forth—not hellfire, but something purer, radiant. They cascaded onto the docks, the river, the cultists’ upturned faces.  

“Gods above,” Elara breathed. “They’ve punctured the Veil.”  

The fire didn’t burn. Where it touched the earth, flowers of crystalline ice bloomed. Where it struck the Chionthar, the water solidified into glowing stepped pools. It was beauty incarnate—and cataclysm incarnate. The cultists shrieked in ecstasy as the energy surged into them. One burst like an overripe fruit, rematerializing as a being of starlight and shadow.  

“They’re ascendants!” I roared. “Stop the elf!”  

Borlus, to his credit, adapted. “Fist! Shatter the formation!” His men flanked left while Elara and I lunged for the leader.  

---

### Chapter IV: Mortals Amidst the Divine  
What followed was less a battle than a desperate ballet. The ascendant cultists wielded light like whips, shearing through stone and steel. Flaming Fist soldiers fell, not to wounds, but to transfigured flesh—one man’s arm turned to glass; another’s scream froze midair as a glyph.  

I parried a blade of solidified moonlight, my own sword—*Dawn’s Echo*, a relic from my stint in Cormyr—flaring with countermagic. Elara hurled a vial of nullifying powder at the sky-keel. The shard cracked, screeching like a dying star.  

The elf leader howled, “You deny us paradise!”  

“Paradise doesn’t need cracking open!” I retorted—a paltry quip, but it bought time. Borlus charged, shield raised, and tackled the elf. The sky-keel shattered.  

The explosion was silent.  

Light drained from the world. For three heartbeats, Baldur’s Gate hung in absolute darkness. Then, with a thunderclap that shattered every window in the Lower City, reality *snapped* back.  

The rift vanished. The cultists collapsed, mortal once more—or dead. The celestial fires cooled into inert crystal. The Tears of Selûne dimmed to their usual glow.  

Dawn found us knee-deep in prismatic debris, tending to the wounded. Borlus clasped my arm, his face ashen. “Never seen magic like that.”  

“Pray you never do again,” I said.  

---

### Chapter V: Lessons Etched in Starlight  
In the aftermath, scholars from Candlekeep swarmed the city. They deduced the cult had tapped into a “veil-nexus”—a rare point where the Material Plane overlaps with the Astral Sea. The sky-keel acted as a lodestone, and Selûne’s Tears, amplified by the Tabernacle’s latent power, fueled the rupture.  

But why revisit this now? Because last night, as I walked Silverymoon’s moonlit streets, I saw a beggar child scrawl a familiar iridescent symbol on the Spire’s steps. The academy dismissed it as vandalism. I recognized the curves—a simplified Choir sigil.  

We forget. We *always* forget.  

---

### Epilogue: The Ceiling of the World  
Some say the gods abandoned Faerûn. Others claim magic is a dying ember. That night in Baldur’s Gate taught me otherwise. The heavens are not passive. They churn, they hunger, and they answer when mortals knock—often with calamity masquerading as grace.  

Now, as I advise Silverymoon’s guilds on tariffs and trade lanes, I heed Harper warnings sharper. I petition the Spellguard to monitor celestial alignments. And when young adventurers boast of taming wild magic, I tell them of the night the sky wept fire—and the fools who tried to drink its tears.  

Restraint is not cowardice. Curiosity without wisdom is a bomb in ivory robes. And vigilance? It’s the quiet art of never letting the stars out of your peripheral vision.  

May Selûne light your path—but may you never invite her fury indoors.
