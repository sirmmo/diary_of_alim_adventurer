In my years wandering Toril—through the blood-soaked sands of Calimshan, the echoing halls of Undermountain, and the whispering forests of Cormanthor—I’ve learned that adventure rarely unfolds as planned. There’s always a twist: a trapdoor where none should be, a smuggler’s code deciphered too late, or, in the case of Westgate, a small gray cat with eyes like moonlit jade.  

Silverymoon’s scholars often ask me, “What’s the most perilous relic you’ve ever recovered?” They expect tales of Netherese orbs or dwarven holy blades. They rarely expect the truth: that one of the most harrowing—and absurd—jobs of my career involved a silver locket, a thieves’ guild war, and a feline who’d have made Cirrush herself proud. So, settle in, friends. Pour yourself something strong. This is the story of the Calimoran Locket, and how I barely escaped Westgate alive, thanks to nine pounds of fur and attitude.  

---

### **The Job: A Scholar, a Sketch, and a Warning Untold**  
It began, as such things often do, with a letter delivered by a harried half-elf courier. The client was Meldara Thistledown, a historian from the Temple of Oghma in Berdusk, whose scrollwork seal trembled with urgency. She sought a relic lost for centuries: the *Eclipse Locket*, forged during Calimshan’s twilight to channel shadow magic. Misused, it could bend light to suffocate entire streets. Purified, it might heal corrupted lands.  

Meldara’s sketches showed a silver pendant shaped like a crescent moon, its edges frayed into smoke. “The Thayan *Red Wizards* seek it,” she wrote. “So do Westgate’s *Night Masks*. Find it first.”  

Westgate. Even now, my jaw tightens. A port city where dock mist tastes of salt and deceit, where guild wars bleed into tavern brawls, and a wrong glance earns a knife between ribs. Still, the coin was good, and Oghma’s followers pay in more than gold—they trade in secrets. Secrets, as any retired adventurer sipping spiced wine in Silverymoon’s guildhalls will tell you, are currency that outlasts empires.  

---

### **Arrival: Smoke, Salt, and a Silent Shadow**  
I reached Westgate at dusk, the sky streaked with lavender and rust. The *Harbor’s End Inn* was as welcoming as a shark’s grin: damp straw mattresses, ale watered down to oblivion, and a barkeep who eyed my coin purse like a lover. That’s where I met *Theron*, a freelance informant with a scar splitting his lip.  

“The locket’s here,” he muttered, leaning close over grimy tankards. “But so’s *Kaleen*—Night Mask lieutenant. Likes to carve her initials into rivals’ bones.”  

I spent three days following whispers: A Calishite smuggler’s ship had docked under false flags. A pawnshop in the *Veiled Ward* bought “shiny trinkets” from drunken sailors. A Thayan mage had inquired about “shadow-play artifacts.” Dead ends all.  

Then, on the fourth night, *she* appeared.  

I was stumbling back to the inn, alley mud sucking at my boots, when a flicker of movement caught my eye. Perched on a rain barrel, a small gray cat stared at me. Not feral—her fur was sleek, her collar frayed but once-expensive velvet. Her eyes held an uncanny awareness, sharp as a tactical map. When I reached to scratch her ears, she darted away, pausing only to flick her tail, as if to say, *“Follow or fall behind.”*  

---

### **The Cat, the Corpse, and the Candlelit Clue**  
Her name, I learned later, was *Ashes*—though to this day, I don’t know who named her. That night, she led me to a corpse in the *Silk Alley*.  

The victim was Theron, throat slit ear-to-ear. No locket, but pinched in his stiff fingers was a candle stub stamped with a crest: a flaming chalice. *The Ember Cup*, a tavern near the docks frequented by Calishite exiles. Coincidence? Not in Westgate.  

Ashes trotted ahead, tail held high like a battle standard. I followed, cursing softly.  

*The Ember Cup* stank of spiced rum and sweat. Sailors bellowed shanties; a minstrel plucked a lute out of tune. And there—slouched in a shadowed booth—was *Vargo*, the smuggler Theron had mentioned, his face leathery from sun and sins.  

Ashes darted past guards, leapt onto Vargo’s table, and stole a grilled fish right off his plate.  

“Hey! You little gutter rat!” Vargo roared, lunging.  

Chaos erupted. Tankards crashed. I seized the moment, sliding beside Vargo as he wrestled the (now smugly fish-devouring) cat.  

“I’ll buy you another,” I said, pressing silver into his palm. “And five more if you tell me about the locket.”  

Vargo’s gaze narrowed. “The Calishite thing? Too late. Sold it yesterday.”  

“To whom?”  

He smirked. “Night Masks. Kaleen’s prize now.”  

He never saw the dart.  

One moment, Vargo was gloating; the next, he choked, clawing at his neck. Dead before he hit the floor. Across the tavern, a hooded figure melted into the crowd. *Thayans*.  

Ashes yowled. I grabbed her and ran.  

---

### **Things Get Worse (They Always Do)**  
The Night Masks’ stronghold squatted in Westgate’s underbelly, accessible only through sewage tunnels—or so I’d heard. But Ashes had her own approach: a crumbling garden wall behind a perfumer’s shop, leading to a rooftop lattice.  

“You’ve done this before,” I muttered as we crept past snoring guards.  

She blinked, whiskers twitching—*obviously*.  

Kaleen’s sanctum reeked of incense and iron. Trophies lined the walls: severed hands, stolen art, and the locket, displayed under glass like a king’s jewel. Ashes prowled the perimeter while I combed for traps.  

Then—footsteps. Kaleen entered, flanked by brutes.  

I froze behind a tapestry. Ashes crouched beneath a table, eyes luminous.  

“*Stupid Thayans,*” Kaleen sneered, lifting the locket. “Think they can snatch *my* prizes? Protect this with your lives.”  

One guard lingered after she left. Ashes leapt onto a shelf, knocking a vial of acid onto his head. His scream brought others running—buying me twenty seconds to smash the glass and snatch the locket...  

Only to realize—*it was a decoy*.  

The true locket was hidden behind a false brick. Clever. But not clever enough. Ashes batted at the wall until I heard a hollow *clink*.  

Then the ceiling exploded.  

---

### **Fire, Fur, and Fury**  
Thayans. Crashing through skylights, fireballs scorching tapestries. Kaleen’s guards charged. Spells lit the room like Sigil’s storms.  

In the fray, Ashes vanished.  

I fought toward the exit, locket searing my pocket like ice. A Thayan—sneering, ringed hands crackling—cornered me. “*The locket, fool. Then your death.*”  

A gray comet shot from the shadows. Ashes latched onto his face, claws digging deep. He screamed, stumbling backward into a brazier. Flames engulfed him.  

She landed gracefully, fur singed but triumphant.  

We fled through gardens and alleys, dodging crossbow bolts. At the docks, I bartered passage on a smuggler’s cog with the last of my gold. Ashes curled on my lap as we cast off, Westgate burning behind us.  

---

### **Epilogue: The Locket’s Fate and a Gift Unasked**  
Meldara purified the locket in Berdusk’s sacred fonts. Thay’s envoys were expelled; the Night Masks fractured without Kaleen (rumor says she fell to a Thayan assassin’s blade).  

I never learned Ashes’ true story. Was she a familiar? A shape-shifted spirit? She vanished two days after we returned, leaving only a mouthful of silver fur on my cloak.  

Sometimes, in Silverymoon’s market, I see a gray cat watching me from a baker’s stall. I smile, toss her a scrap of salmon. She never stays.  

Adventure is a tapestry of chaos, friends. You plan for dragons and get a cat. You chase artifacts and find kinship in fur and fury. I keep no trophies from my travels—except a singed scrap of velvet collar, tucked in my desk.  

If you visit Westgate, stay vigilant. Drink cautiously. And if a cat beckons you into shadows...  

*Follow.*
