Let me state this plainly: I am not, by trade, a thief. A negotiator? Certainly. A strategist? Ask the Zhentarim agents who still flinch at the mention of the High Moor Campaign. But a *purse-snatcher*? A *sneak-thief*? Hardly. Yet there I was, fifteen years ago, crouched behind a marble statue of some long-dead Masked Lord in Waterdeep’s Sea Ward, watching a half-elven bard attempt to seduce a suit of enchanted plate armor while our target—the sealed plans for a *literal city-destroying arcane cannon*—sat gleaming in a vault behind him.  

This, dear reader, is why one must never accept contracts after three pints of Evermead.  

### The Job: Simplicity Itself (Or So I Thought)  
The offer arrived via a sleek raven bearing the sigil of the Harpers—or a convincing forgery thereof. The message was crystalline in its urgency: *Infiltrate the Villa of Lord Kelthas Obrion, disgraced mage-in-exile from Thay. Retrieve the schematics for “Project Fulcrum,” a weapon designed to destabilize the bedrock beneath Waterdeep itself. Do not open the scroll case. Do not engage Obrion. Burn this missive.*  

A noble task, if grim. Waterdeep had been my home for nearly a decade following the Trollpurge Wars. Letting it sink into the Trackless Sea because some Red Wizard’s bruised ego demanded vengeance struck me as... poor form. The pay was negligible, the risks immense. I nearly refused.  

Then I met my partner.  

### Enter Fenrik the “Feywild Adjacent”  
The Harpers’ safehouse—a tannery reeking of vinegar and desperation—introduced me to Fenrik Loonshade, a bard of such spectacular eccentricity that I initially assumed he was a distraction. His attire was a masterpiece of chaos: peacock feathers sewn into a patchwork doublet, boots mismatched (one snakeskin, one what appeared to be deep dragonhide), and a lute decorated with tiny bells that chimed with his every twitch.  

“Call me Fenrik!” he’d proclaimed, bowing so low his nose brushed the sawdust floor. “Or ‘The Tempest in Teapot’! Or ‘The Duke of Dissonance’! Though only ex-lovers use the latter. Bitter, the lot of them. Now! Shall we discuss our upcoming *duet of delinquency*?”  

I should have fled. Gods know Entreri would’ve knifed the fool mid-sentence and finished the job alone. But Harper debts linger like stink-spray on wool, so I endured.  

### The Plan (Or Lack Thereof)  
Fenrik’s “strategy” unfolded over two bottles of Amnian red:  
1. He’d pose as an itinerant musician seeking patronage from Obrion.  
2. I’d scale the villa’s western wall during the ensuing “audition.”  
3. Retrieve the case from the vault (third sublevel, behind a portrait of Szass Tam riding a nightmare—***tacky***).  
4. Escape via pre-Enchanted smoke pellets (“Pepperpops!” Fenrik chirped. “My own recipe!”)  

I noted gaping flaws (e.g., no reconnaissance, no contingency routes, no definition of what constituted an “audition” with a paranoid Red Wizard). Fenrik waved away concerns like gnats.  

“Improvisation, my stone-footed friend! The soul of artistry! Besides”—he winked, plucking a minor chord that made my teeth ache—“I’ve a *knack* for distraction.”  

### The Performance: Act I – Disaster in D Sharp  
Obrion’s villa was a statement piece in existential dread: black marble, wrought-iron gargoyles, and a pervasive smell of burnt hair (failed experiments, presumably). Fenrik pranced to the gate, lute in hand, and delivered a soliloquy so florid it momentarily stunned the guards into admitting him. I crept along the rooftops, leather-gloved hands silent on rain-slicked stone.  

Then: Fenrik’s “audition.”  

Through a stained-glass window, I watched him launch into a ballad titled *Ode to the Unraveling of Sanity*. Within moments:  
- A candelabra sprouted legs and tap-danced.  
- Two guards began weeping uncontrollably.  
- Lord Obrion himself vibrated with fury, his goatee smoking actual embers.  

**The vault door creaked open.**  

So far, functional chaos.  

### Interlude: Why Bards Should Not Cast *Polymorph*  
The vault was obsidian and cold iron–standard anti-scrying fare. The scroll case waited on a pedestal, glowing faintly violet. No traps. No guards. No Szass Tam portrait (Fenrik’s sole error). I seized the case.  

Then: a deafening ***FWUMPH***.  

Fenrik, cornered by Obrion’s summoned magma mephit, had opted for creative problem-solving. Specifically: casting *Polymorph* on the creature. Now, rather than a fiery assassin, a confused-looking alpaca stood in the parlor, chewing thoughtfully on Obrion’s favorite rug.  

“See?” Fenrik crowed, dodging a firebolt. “Harmless!”  

The spell, however, required concentration.  

### Act II: When the Alpaca Exploded  
I was halfway to the rendezvous point when the backlash hit. Fenrik’s concentration shattered—likely due to Obrion throwing a *Crown of Stars* at his head—reverting the alpaca *mid-combustion*. The resulting explosion took out Obrion’s eastern turret, six guards, and (crucially) the villa’s main gate.  

“TIMELY EXIT, YES?!” Fenrik shrieked, sprinting past me, lute miraculously intact, beard singed. The case burned in my grip—enchanted to grow heavier with distance from its creator. Obrion’s roar shook the streets.  

We plunged into the Dock Ward’s labyrinth, Fenrik dropping Pepperpops like festival favors. They emitted pink smoke smelling of burnt roses and existential regret. By the seventh alley, we’d lost pursuit—but not the scroll case.  

### The Scroll Case Incident (Or: How Bards Break Seals)  
“I’ve an idea,” Fenrik gasped, leaning against a fishmonger’s stall.  

“No.”  

“But my charm—”  

“**No.**”  

He unscrewed the lute’s peghead, producing a lockpick shaped like a mermaid. “Just a peek?”  

One does not “peek” at Thayan weapons designs. But before I could wrestle the case away, Fenrik jammed his pick into the arcane lock. With a sound like shattering crystal, the seal burst—followed by an earsplitting **shriek** as the case spat out:  

A) Schematics for a resonating bedrock-bore.  
B) A coiled viper made of shadow (predictably venomous).  
C) An illusory projection of Szass Tam’s disembodied head, howling threats in High Netherese.  

**D) All of the above.**  

### Act III: Escape Courtesy of Avian Transformation  
The viper struck my boot (thank Tymora for dwarven craftsmanship). Szass Tam’s head recited fourteen stanzas of a lich-lord’s lament (a *shocking* number of rhymes for “mortals”). Fenrik, ever *practical*, cast *Enthralling Performance* on the snake.  

It worked disturbingly well. The viper coiled, swaying, as Fenrik crooned *The Ballad of Star-Crossed Vipers*.  

“Now *fly*, you magnificent oaf!” he yelled, tossing me a feather. A *Feather Token of Airwalking*, to be precise. Ingenious, if belated.  

We soared above the docks as Obrion’s remaining guards flooded the alley. The case remained blissfully closed—this time by a length of twine and Fenrik’s yellow neckerchief.  

“Told you improvisation was key!” he shouted over the wind.  

“That was *luck*!”  

“Luck is improvisation’s bastard cousin!”  

The Harpers met us at Pier 12, cloaked and joyless. They snatched the case, handed over a sack of gold (mostly copper), and vanished. Fenrik tipped his feathered cap to their shadows.  

### Epilogue: Lessons Loudly Learned  
Lord Obrion fled Waterdeep three days later, his plans foiled. Fenrik composed an anthem about the incident (*The Viper, The Villain, & The Vault of Vexations*), which became a ramble-shanty in dockside taverns. I developed a permanent flinch at the sound of lute strings.  

So why share this? Because in Silverymoon’s council halls, I watch guildmasters scoff at “unpredictable assets”—bards, wild mages, or anyone refusing standardized tactics. But Waterdeep taught me this: Chaos is not the enemy of order; it is its sparring partner. Without Fenrik’s madness, Obrion’s cannon might’ve shattered the City of Splendors.  

Would I partner with Fenrik again? I’d rather juggle starving rust monsters. But should you find yourself amidst smoke, screaming liches, and a bard tuning his lute mid-freefall?  

**Listen.** Not to his *plan*. To the chaos. There’s music in it.  

Just pray it’s not in the key of catastrophe.  

—Alim Kors, Written in the Gemfire Inn, Silverymoon, amidst the merciful absence of lutes
