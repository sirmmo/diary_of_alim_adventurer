Thay. Even now, decades later, the name feels like ash on my tongue. A land of crimson sands and bleached bones, where the air hums with necromantic energy and ambition curdles into tyranny. I had ventured there reluctantly in my youth, drawn by whispers of artifacts buried beneath the ruins of Delhumide—a fool’s errand, in hindsight. But what I witnessed in that forsaken place reshaped my understanding of faith, sacrifice, and the fragile power of light in a world steeped in shadow.  

It began in the dead of night, near the banks of the sluggish Thazarim River. My companions—a Zhentarim sellsword and a half-mad alchemist from Amn—had long since abandoned me, spooked by the eerie wails that echoed from the crumbling ziggurats. I pressed on alone, driven by stubbornness more than courage, until I stumbled upon a sight that froze my blood: a circle of Red Wizards, their shaved heads gleaming in the moonlight, channeling a ritual into a massive obsidian monolith. Spirits writhed within the stone like trapped serpents, and the ground itself pulsed with a sickly purple light. They were tearing open a gate. Not merely to the Lower Planes, but to something *older*. Something even fiends feared.  

Before I could retreat, a figure emerged from the dunes like a tempest given form. A paladin. Not just any knight in polished armor, but a weathered warrior clad in scorched plate, her tabard singged and stained. She bore no insignia, no crest of a famed order—only a simple silver amulet of Lathander dangling from her neck. Her eyes blazed with a fervor that bordered on madness, yet her voice, when she shouted, was clear as a temple bell: *“By the Morninglord’s grace, this ends* now*!”*  

The Red Wizards laughed. Arrogance is their birthright, after all. They unleashed waves of necrotic energy, skeletal thralls, and a vortex of screaming souls. But the paladin did not waver. She moved with the precision of a dancer, her sword—a humble blade devoid of enchantment—flaring to life with golden flames. Where it struck, the undead crumbled to dust, and dark magic recoiled like a whipped cur. I watched, crouched behind a skeletal tree, as she carved a path toward the monolith, her prayers rising above the din: *“Ignite the dark, Lathander! Let your dawn break even here!”*  

Then came the miracle.  

As the lead wizard, a gaunt man with eyes like smoking coals, began a final incantation, the paladin plunged her blade into the earth. Light erupted—not the harsh glare of a spell, but a warm, *living* radiance. It spilled across the sand like liquid sun, washing over the monolith. The stone shuddered. The trapped spirits within it ceased their wailing and turned their translucent faces upward, as if beholding a sky they’d forgotten. Then, slowly, they dissolved into motes of light, rising like embers toward the stars.  

The wizards recoiled, screaming as their ritual unraveled. One burst into flames; another withered to a husk, his stolen life-force ripped away. But the paladin paid them no heed. She knelt before the monolith, hands pressed to its now-dull surface, her own light flickering. The cost was written in the sudden age lining her face, the blood seeping from beneath her armor. She had poured her very soul into that strike.  

I finally found my courage—or my shame—and rushed to her side. She was dying, her breath shallow, yet she smiled. *“Thay’s shadows endure,”* she rasped, clutching my arm, *“but so do we. Tell them… tell them* light leaves no vacuum. *Where it falls, hope roots.”*  

Then, as dawn’s first rays breached the horizon, she went still. But here is the part that defies all logic, all my years of scraping by in a world of cheap tricks and cheaper miracles: her body did not decay. It simply… faded. Dissolving into a shower of golden sparks that spiraled upward and vanished. Even the Red Wizards’ ashes were swallowed by the wind, leaving no trace of the battle but the scarred monolith—now inert, a mere relic of thwarted ambition.  

I left Thay soon after, the paladin’s amulet clutched in my hand (I’d found it resting atop her discarded armor, warm to the touch). It hangs in my study now, in Silverymoon, a reminder that courage is not the absence of fear, but the choice to stand when standing seems absurd. That lone warrior, nameless and unheralded, did what armies could not: she struck a blow against the infinite entropy of evil, not with grand alliances or artifacts, but with the one weapon Thay could never corrupt—unbreakable faith.  

The Red Wizards still rule Thay, of course. Darkness persists. But on quiet mornings, when sunlight spills through my window and glints off that silver amulet, I swear I hear her voice in the stillness: *“Light leaves no vacuum.”* And I believe her. For in the end, Thay’s shadows are not invincible—merely waiting for the next dawn, and the next paladin, to remind them they are fleeting.
