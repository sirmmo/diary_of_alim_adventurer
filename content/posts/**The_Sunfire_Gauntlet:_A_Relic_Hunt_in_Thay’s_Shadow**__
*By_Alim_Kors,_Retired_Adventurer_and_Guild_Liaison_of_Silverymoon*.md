### I. The Call to Thay: A Land of Blood and Dust  
It’s been twenty summers since I set foot in Thay, but the memory of its blood-red skies and the scent of scorched earth still lingers in my nightmares. Most sane folk avoid the place, and for good reason—Thay is a necrocracy ruled by the Red Wizards, where ambition is etched in bone and fueled by enslaved souls. Yet in my youth, I was foolish enough to accept a contract there. Not for gold, but to stop a cult of Myrkul from unleashing an artifact capable of draining life from entire regions—the *Sunfire Gauntlet*.  

The job came through Elia Mornshield, a Harper agent based in the Sword Coast. She’d intercepted whispers of the Cult of the Final Breath, a Myrkulite sect operating in Thay’s western marshlands. The Red Wizards, typically possessive of their territory, had ignored the cult’s activities; some even supplied them, hoping to weaponize the Gauntlet against rival enclaves. Elia’s missive was blunt: *“Retrieve the Gauntlet before the Thayans claim it. Destroy it if you must. Do not let it fall into *any* hands.”*  

I’d just wrapped up a messy affair in the High Forest—one involving a treant’s misplaced wrath and a stolen gem—and was eager for fresh work. But even I knew Thay demanded allies. Which is how I found myself saddled with Arvid Vance, a paladin of Lathander.  

### II. The Paladin’s Burden  
Arvid was pure Dawnbringer stock: polished armor, a tabard bright as summer dawn, and a moral compass sharper than his bastard sword. We met in the Thayan port of Amruthar, where the air reeked of sulfur and defeat. Refugees shuffled through markets selling charms to ward off "the Red Curse"—a colloquial term for Thayan oppression.  

“Kors,” Arvid greeted me, gripping my forearm with gauntleted force. “I’ve heard of your work. You’re… pragmatic.”  
*A polite way of saying I bend rules*, I thought. “And you’re here to ensure I don’t ‘pragmatize’ this into heresy?”  
“I’m here to ensure the Gauntlet is purified, not exploited,” he corrected. “Lathander’s light won’t suffer relics of undeath to exist.”  

Our partnership was uneasy from the start. Arvid saw the mission as a holy crusade; I saw it as a heist with higher stakes. Thay had no tolerance for outsiders—especially do-gooders—so we disguised ourselves as mercenaries seeking work with the Cult. The ruse required merciless acting. In the Amruthar slums, we let slavers assume we were hunting escaped thralls. Arvid’s jaw tightened the entire time, but he played his part.  

“Evil flourishes when good men tolerate lesser evils,” he muttered that night as we camped in the Raithe swamp.  
“Evil also flourishes when good men starve because they refused to dirty their hands,” I shot back.  
He didn’t argue. We needed each other.  

### III. Into the Mire  
The Cult of the Final Breath operated in Dreadmurk Hollow, a fenland where willows wept black sap and the water shimmered with necrotic energy. Local guides refused to take us, muttering about “bone-kissers” and “the Unseen Prince.” We hired a Thayan tracker instead—Velya, a shadar-kai exile with a grudge against her former kin.  

“The Cult’s leader is a defector from the Red Wizards,” Velya told us as we poled through stagnant canals. “Zass Tam. He believes Myrkul will elevate him beyond lichdom.”  
Arvid scowled. “Blasphemy layered on blasphemy.”  
I kept my thoughts to myself. Tam’s ambition was our opportunity; turncoats are always paranoid.  

The Hollow’s entrance was a crumbling ziggurat, half-sunk in the muck. Corpses hung from chains around its perimeter, their flesh pickled in alchemical brine. Arvid murmured a prayer; I nocked an arrow. Inside, the air hummed with flies and the greasy feel of necromancy.  

### IV. Dungeons and Dilemmas  
The cultists were complacent, relying on the swamp’s lethality as their first defense. We infiltrated the ziggurat’s catacombs unchallenged—until we stumbled into a ritual chamber. Five acolytes chanted over a bound prisoner, their daggers poised to flay his skin for a “living map” to the Gauntlet’s vault.  

Arvid surged forward, blade blazing with solar fury. “In Lathander’s name, *cease*!”  
Chaos erupted. I felled two cultists with arrows to the throat while Arvid’s greatsword sheared through bone. The prisoner—a Thayan farmer—collapsed, shuddering. Arvid knelt to heal him, leaving his back exposed. A cultist lunged with a poisoned stiletto; my arrow took them through the eye.  

“Thank you,” Arvid said stiffly afterward.  
“Save it. We’re not done.”  

The farmer led us to a hidden stairwell, whispering that the Gauntlet lay in the “Chamber of Eclipses,” protected by a curse only Myrkul’s favored could bypass. As we descended, the walls shifted from stone to fused bone. Arvid’s holy light dimmed, choked by the void.  

“This place rejects life,” he said grimly.  
“Tell me something I don’t know,” I replied, testing each step for traps.  

### V. Zass Tam’s Gambit  
We found Zass Tam in the Chamber of Eclipses, a domed room where faint starlight filtered through cracks in the ceiling. The *Sunfire Gauntlet* hovered atop an obsidian pedestal—a relic of burnished gold encrusted with amber crystals that pulsed like a dying heart. Tam, skeletally thin with eyes like smoldering coals, smiled as we entered.  

“Ah. The mercenary and the missionary,” he rasped. “You’re too late. The Gauntlet thirsts, and it *will* drink.”  

He raised a hand, and pillars of shadow disgorged undead horrors—stitched-together abominations of swamp creatures and humanoid corpses. Arvid charged, his blade carving radiant arcs through the darkness. I targeted Tam, but the necromancer shielded himself with a wall of force.  

“Foolish paladin!” Tam spat. “The Gauntlet isn’t a tool—it’s a *trap*. Even now, it feeds on your light!”  
Horrified, I realized the amber crystals were brightening—draining Arvid’s divine energy with every spell he cast.  

### VI. The Gauntlet’s Truth  
Velya’s warning echoed: *“The Gauntlet doesn’t steal life—it steals *purpose*. It turns light to shadow, hope to despair.”*  

I cursed. Tam hadn’t wanted the Gauntlet for himself—he wanted Arvid to *fuel* it. While the paladin dueled undead, I scaled the chamber walls, dodging spectral tendrils. Tam, engrossed in his ritual, didn’t notice me until my dagger pricked his spine.  

“Release the barrier,” I hissed.  
He laughed. “Kill me, and the Gauntlet detonates. But collaborate, and I’ll share its power!”  
Arvid roared, staggering as the Gauntlet’s drain intensified. His armor cracked; his sword flickered.  

In the end, the choice was Arvid’s. As Tam gloated, the paladin did the unthinkable: he impaled himself on a zombie’s claw, collapsing within inches of the Gauntlet. The amber crystals flared, gorged on his surrendered light—then overloaded.  

Tam screamed as the chamber imploded with purifying fire.  

### VII. The Cost of Dawn  
I dragged Arvid from the rubble, his body scorched but breathing. The Gauntlet lay shattered, its magic spent. Velya vanished with a shadar-kai’s typical discretion, leaving us to flee the collapsing ziggurat.  

For three days, I nursed Arvid through fever and infection in a smuggler’s den outside Amruthar. He awoke gaunt but resolute.  
“Why save me?” he asked hoarsely. “I was ready to die.”  
I shrugged. “Someone’s got to annoy the Harpers with reports.”  

We never spoke of Thay again, but the mission changed us both. Arvid returned to Lathander’s service, though he admitted the Gauntlet’s curse left him unable to channel the Morninglord’s full power. As for me? I learned even pragmatism has limits.  

### Epilogue: Relics and Regrets  
The Harpers lauded our success, but Thay festered. Zass Tam was one of countless power-hungry spellcasters, and the Red Wizards eventually replicated the Gauntlet’s magic in their Battledawn experiments. As for Arvid, he fell defending Elturel during the *Descent*—a hero’s end, fittingly.  

In Silverymoon’s guild halls, young adventurers beg for tales of Thay. I warn them: artifacts like the Sunfire Gauntlet aren’t prizes. They’re levers for tyrants—or traps for fools. These days, I prefer fighting with policy scrolls rather than blades, trusting subtler tools to keep the shadows at bay.  

*Light redeems even the darkest places*, Arvid once said. But in Thay, the light we brought was just another flicker swallowed by the dark.
