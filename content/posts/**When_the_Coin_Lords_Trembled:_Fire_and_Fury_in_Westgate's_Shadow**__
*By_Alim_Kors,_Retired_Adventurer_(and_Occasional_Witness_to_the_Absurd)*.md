For all its flaws—and, gods know, Westgate has flaws aplenty—the city has a way of searing itself into your memory. I’ve walked the Sword Coast from Luskan to Calimport, but nowhere else have I seen such a perfect marriage of cruelty and beauty. The harbor at sunset, its waters turned to liquid copper by the dying light. The scent of saffron and salt mingling with the ever-present stench of fish guts and intrigue. It’s a place where fortunes bloom and rot between sunrise and sundown. Yet of all the madness I witnessed there, nothing haunts me like the night the very earth rebelled against the Coin Lords’ schemes.  

### The Calm Before the Inferno  
It was the Year of the Tankard, 1370 if memory serves. I’d been hired to discreetly escort a spice merchant’s daughter back from a kidnapping—a tedious affair involving the Fire Knives guild’s lesser enforcers and a truly atrocious ransom poem. Negotiations had concluded, the gold had changed hands (with a few discreetly pilfered extra coins for my troubles), and I found myself nursing a flagon of Westgate’s infamous “Siren’s Kiss” ale in a dockside tavern called *The Leering Mermaid*. Outside, the air hung thick and still, the kind of humid silence that makes your shirt cling to your back like a nervous child. Even the gulls had gone quiet.  

Then the tremors began.  

### The Ground Speaks  
At first, we dismissed it. Westgate’s foundations are notoriously unstable—cellars flood, cobblestones shift, and smugglers’ tunnels collapse monthly. But the trembling didn’t stop. The tavern’s lanterns swung like pendulums, and I watched a crack split the plaster wall behind the bar, chewing through a lewd mural of Umberlee with unsettling hunger. Within minutes, the city’s hidden enemy made itself known: Mount Thulbanar, the long-dormant volcano brooding west of the city, had woken with a roar that shook the Dragon’s Head Peninsula to its roots.  

Ash fell first. Not the gentle gray snow of a campfire’s dying embers, but thick, cloying flakes that tasted of sulfur and burnt bones. Then came the rivers of fire.  

### Thieves in Hell’s Light  
Here’s where the tale twists, like a knife slipped between ribs. Most assume thieves’ guilds thrive solely in shadows. But disaster strips men bare, and what I saw that night was… unexpected. Near Knuckle Street, where the Fire Knives and their rivals, the Night Masks, conducted their bloodiest feuds, the ground ruptured. A seam split open, vomiting molten rock into the Guilder’s Market—a pit of snakes if ever there was one. Stalls of black-market goods ignited like torches. A crate of stolen Evereskan wine exploded, showering the street in glass and blue flames.  

And there they were: members of both guilds, momentarily united by primal terror. A Fire Knife enforcer—a hulking brute whose face I’d last seen glowering at me over a garrote—hauled a Night Mask courier from the path of a lava flow. A Night Mask archer, perched on a buckling roof, shouted warnings as fire geysers burst through the cobbles below. One resourceful scoundrel even commandeered a stolen water wagon, dousing flames engulfing a tenement where rival guilds stored their… less legally ambiguous merchandise.   

They fought the mountain, not each other. For an hour, maybe two, greed and vengeance meant nothing. Survival was the only currency that mattered.  

### The Irony of Cooperation  
Oh, don’t mistake this for some trite ballad about brotherhood. The moment the earth stilled and the lava cooled into twisted blackglass sculptures, the knives came out again. I watched a Fire Knife lieutenant drive a dagger into the spine of the very man who’d helped him tip over a burning cart moments prior. “Business is business,” he snarled, wiping the blade on his victim’s silks. The Night Masks retaliated by torching a Fire Knife gambling den before dawn.  

But here’s the rub: For all their posturing, both guilds lost more that night to Thulbanar’s wrath than to each other in a decade of feuding. Warehouses of stolen treasures? Melted into slag. Networks of tunnels? Filled with magma or collapsed. Informants? Dead, dispersed, or delightedly exploiting the chaos to vanish with their ill-gotten gains. The mountain hadn’t just erupted—it had levied the fairest tax Westgate had ever seen.  

### Reflections in Blackglass  
These days, when guildmasters here in Silverymoon drone on about territorial disputes or “necessary violence,” I think of Westgate under that ashen sky. I recall how fire exposed the fragility of their games. Guilds hoard power like dragons hoard gold, forgetting that the ground beneath their feet isn’t a chessboard—it’s the *dragon*.  

So, the next time you hear whispers of shadow wars or smuggler’s quarrels, remember Mount Thulbanar. Remember how quickly coin and contracts crumble before the earth’s indifference. And if you’re wise, dear reader, you’ll invest in a good pair of running boots—and geography lessons.  

After all, nature doesn’t negotiate.  

*-Alim Kors divides his time between advising Silverymoon’s less insufferable guild leaders and avoiding invitations to “just one last job.” His memoir, “*Nearly Murdered: A Professional’s Guide to Staying Alive,*” remains tragically unwritten.*
