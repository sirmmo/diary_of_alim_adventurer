Thay reeks of saffron and secrets. Even now, decades later, the memory of that sweltering tenday in Eltabbar makes my palms sweat—and not merely from the cloying humidity that clings to the Red Wizards’ domain like a jealous lover. No, it was the day I met *her*: a moon elf woman whose very presence in that viper’s nest of blood magic and tyranny should’ve been impossible. Yet there she stood, draped in plain dun traveler’s robes, bargaining for dried figs at a spice bazaar like she hadn’t just rewritten every rule of survival in the Scarlet Realm.  

I’d gone to Thay under the Guild’s auspices—a “diplomatic retrieval” of a Waterdhavian merchant foolish enough to gamble with Zulkir slaves. Routine, if such a word applies to a nation where the cobblestones sing dirges and children play with enchanted scorpions. My contact, a gaunt Thayan scribe with eyes like stale wine, had muttered coordinates to a teahouse called *The Gilded Sting*. “Ask for the Sapphire Lady,” he’d hissed, pressing a slip of vellum into my hand. “And don’t stare when you see her.”  

Naturally, I stared.  

She sat in the teahouse’s shadowed corner, hood lowered just enough to reveal the sharp planes of her face—skin like polished moonlight, hair the color of storm clouds threaded with silver. But it was her eyes that arrested me: twin chips of glacier ice, assessing me with a calm that felt more dangerous than any spell. Around her neck hung a pendant—a teardrop of moonstone clasped in mithral vines. Recognition punched the breath from my lungs.  

*The Emerald Envoy.*  

Even in the North, we’d heard whispers. Lysandra Therysso, once a high diplomat of Evereska, now a phantom who brokered ceasefires between rebel factions and the Zulkirs. Some called her a traitor to elfkind; others, the last sane voice in a land drunk on necromancy. And here she was, sipping cardamom tea in Eltabbar’s underbelly as if it were a summer garden in Everlund.  

“You’re shorter than I expected, Master Kors,” she said, her voice low and honeyed, yet edged like a scalpel. “Sit. The thayan *flame-roasted* leaves are surprisingly delicate, despite the theatrical presentation.” She gestured to the teapot, where blue fire licked its base.  

The meeting should’ve been simple: Lysandra held information about the merchant’s location; I’d trade gold for it. But Thay thrives on complexity. Midway through our negotiation, the teahouse door exploded inward.  

Szass Tam’s lackeys, clad in crimson scale mail, flooded the room—not for me, but for *her*. A “disinvitation,” their captain sneered, as enchanted manacles glowed in his grip. Lysandra didn’t flinch. “It seems,” she murmured, setting down her cup, “Nymar Fellsoul remains as graceless a host as ever.”  

What happened next… well, let’s say my Guild contract didn’t cover “aiding fugitive elves in armed insurrection.” But when a squad of Thayan soldiers levels wands at an unarmed woman, something in my fool’s principles twitches. I tipped the flaming teapot into the captain’s lap.  

Chaos erupted. Lysandra moved like quicksilver, disarming two guards with strikes so precise they seemed choreographed. Her pendant flared—a pulse of argent light that shattered the manacles. We fled through the kitchens, dodging spectral hounds and a rather cross fire elemental, our exit aided by… spices. (Pro tip: Throw a handful of dreamroot powder at pursuing wizards. It won’t stop them, but they’ll hallucinate you turning into a giant singing turnip—an image too humiliating to report to their superiors.)  

We sheltered in a dysentery-ridden ferry bound for the Lake of Steam, crouched between reeking barrels of pickled eels. Lysandra, remarkably unruffled, offered me a pear from her satchel. “You fight well for a man who claims to prefer ‘quiet retirement.’”  

I stared at her. “You *knew* they’d come.”  

“Szass Tam intercepts all foreign envoys. I needed someone… deniable.” Her smile was a blade wrapped in silk. “Your Guildmaster owes me a favor.”  

Ah. Politics.  

For three days, we navigated Thay’s schemes. Lysandra was hunting her own prize: a ledger proving the Zulkirs were trafficking fey spirits from the High Forest. Our paths tangled—mine to save a fool merchant, hers to fracture the slave trade’s spine. We raided a bone golem factory (a story involving animated skeletons and bad puns I’ll spare you), and yes, I retrieved the merchant—though he’d lost three toes to a “creative interrogation” involving crab lice.  

But the true revelation was Lysandra. Watching her parley with a Tharchion in one breath and poison his wine in the next (non-lethally, she assured me), I grasped why she terrified both allies and enemies. She understood Thay’s rot because she’d steeped in it willingly, diving into the muck to plant seeds of rebellion. “Change grows slowly here,” she told me as we parted at the border, moonstone pendant glittering under a starless sky. “Like lichen on a tomb. But it *grows*.”  

Years later, when Thay’s civil war erupted and whispers named an elusive “Ghost of Eltabbar” as a key strategist, I nursed an old man’s secret grin in Silverymoon’s taverns. Lysandra’s ledger had stoked the flames, they said. The merchant? He opened a bakery in Daggerford. Sells terrible fig tarts.  

Thay still reeks of saffron and secrets. But sometimes, in the gathered shadow where light seems impossible, a sapphire flicker cuts through the crimson dark.
