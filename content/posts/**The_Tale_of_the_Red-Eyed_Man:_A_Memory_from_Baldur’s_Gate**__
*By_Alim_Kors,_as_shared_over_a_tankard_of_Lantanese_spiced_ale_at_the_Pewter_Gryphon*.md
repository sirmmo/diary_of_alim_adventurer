### I. The Smoke and the Storm  
The Lower City of Baldur’s Gate reeks of two things when the rain comes: wet stone and revelation. It’s the kind of place where secrets slither out of damp alleyways when the lanterns gutter. I hadn’t planned to linger there long—just a resupply stop before heading north to scout rumors of a displacer beast pack near the High Moor. But fate, as it often does, tucked a story into my path like a blade slipped beneath a door. 

I took shelter that night at the *Blushing Mermaid*, a tavern whose reputation for cheap ale and quieter corners suited my mood. The place was a symphony of rough edges: the sour tang of spilled wine, the creak of warped floorboards, and the low hum of conversations not meant for daylight. I’d claimed a table near the hearth—close enough to feel its warmth, far enough to watch the room without invitation. That’s when I heard her. 

An old woman, hunched like a question mark over her mug, was whispering to a wide-eyed dockhand. Her voice was a rasp, the sort that clawed its way through years of pipe smoke and bad luck. 

*"...red eyes,"* she said, tapping a gnarled finger on the table. *"Not like a devil’s, mind you—deeper. Like coals buried in ash. And his shadow… it* moved *when he didn’t."* 

The dockhand scoffed, but his knuckles whitened around his tankard. Across the room, a tiefling in a merchant’s embroidered coat turned sharply, as if the words had bitten him. 

I’d seen that reaction before. Fear, yes—but beneath it, the uneasy thrill of a truth too strange to dismiss.

### II. The Half-Daemon’s Bargain  
The woman—Marla, I later learned—spun a grim tapestry with her words. Two nights prior, she’d been scavenging the sewers near the Basilisk Gate (a questionable vocation, but Baldur’s Gate has always fed its rats). There, in a chamber stinking of stagnant water and burnt herbs, she’d stumbled upon a gathering. Five figures in ink-black cloaks, circling a stone slab where a man lay bound. 

No—*not* a man. 

*"His skin was gray as grave dirt,"* Marla whispered, leaning in. *"And those eyes… they peeled the dark right open. The others called him ‘Voras.’ Offered him vials of blood—their own, by the cuts on their arms. Bargaining for something. Power, maybe. Or vengeance."* 

And then the shadow. *"It slithered off the wall like oil. Wrapped around one fool’s throat while Voras laughed. Not a human laugh. More like… stones grinding in a tomb."* 

The dockhand interrupted, half-drunk bravado in his voice. *"Daemon stories. Flaming fools summon what they can’t control."* 

But Marla shook her head. *"Wasn’t summoned. He was* born *like that. One of the cloaks said it—‘your mother’s blood betrays you.’ Voras tore his throat out for that."* 

Ah. A half-daemon. Rare, even in the Gate. Most don’t survive infancy; mortal flesh buckling under Abyssal lineage. Those who do… well. They either master their hunger or let it master them. I’d crossed paths with such a soul once, years ago in Calimshan—a mercenary whose touch could wither roses to dust. We’d parted without blades drawn, but I still dream of his hollow, hurricane eyes. 

### III. Interlude: The Weight of Lineage  
You’ll note I don’t say "half-demon." There’s a difference. Fiends of the Abyss are chaos given teeth; demons revel in destruction for its own sake. Daemons—yugoloths, if you prefer the scholar’s term—are colder. Calculated. They trade souls like merchants trade spice, thriving on despair distilled into contract. A half-daemon child isn’t born raving; it’s born *hungry*. For power. For order. For a way to twist the world’s crookedness into a weapon. 

I’d wager Voras’s cultists didn’t understand that when they summoned him. They saw a tool. A key to unlock some petty ambition. But daemonspawn don’t make tools; they make gambits. 

### IV. The Chase Through Storm-Slick Streets  
Marla’s tale might’ve ended there—a tavern ghost story—if not for the scream. 

It came from the alley behind the Mermaid, sharp as broken glass. The tavern stilled; even the drunkard by the bar froze mid-slur. Then came the sound of claws on cobblestones. 

I’d like to say I charged out nobly. Truth? I hesitated. Retirement had gentled my reflexes, and Silverymoon’s politics had left me softer than I cared to admit. But old habits—older than guild meetings, older than my creaking knees—propelled me into the rain. 

The alley was a mouth of shadows. A young woman—human, dressed in a scribe’s ink-stained robes—pressed herself against a barrel, paralyzed. Ten paces from her, *he* stood. 

Voras. 

Marla’s description hadn’t done him justice. Seven feet tall, slender as a whip, with skin like tarnished silver. His eyes weren’t just red; they smoldered, casting crimson light across the puddles at his feet. His shadow writhed independently, snapping at the scribe like a hound on a chain. 

*"I require the ledger,"* he hissed. His voice was silk wrapped around jagged iron. *"The Blackhand shipments. You will give it."* 

The scribe shook her head, mute with terror. Voras raised a hand—long fingers tipped in black talons—and his shadow lunged— 

—only to recoil as my blade met it. Moonsteel, forged in Evereska. The shadow screamed (a sound I felt in my bones, not my ears), and Voras turned those hellfire eyes on me. 

*"An ant with a thorn,"* he sneered. *"Walk away, mortal. Your soul is too stale to interest me."* 

We fought. 

I won’t romanticize it. It was brutal, clumsy, fueled more by desperation than skill. His shadow lashed like a scourge, scoring grooves in the brickwork. He moved with predatory grace, untroubled by the rain-slick stones. I bled—a gash across my thigh, another scoring my forearm. 

But Voras faltered when my blade grazed his ribs. His blood wasn’t red. It was *void*-black, shimmering with tiny motes of light, like stars drowned in ink. And it burned. Where the droplets struck the ground, the cobbles cracked and crumbled to ash. 

The scribe used the moment to run. I’d bought her five seconds. Maybe six. Enough. 

Voras vanished shortly after—not in smoke or flame, but by stepping sideways into his own shadow, leaving behind only the stench of ozone and decay. 

### V. Revelations in the Aftermath  
The scribe, once coaxed from behind a fishmonger’s stall, told me the rest. Voras was hunting records of illicit shipments from Baldur’s Gate to Amn—weapons, alchemical reagents, possibly slaves. The cultists weren’t zealots; they were middlemen. Fools who’d brokered a pact with a half-daemon and realized, too late, that their "ally" had his own agenda. 

*"He wants to destroy the network,"* she whispered, shivering under my cloak. *"Wipe out everyone involved. Not for justice. Because they… they insulted him. Called him a mongrel."* 

Pride. The daemon’s curse. More potent than any hunger. 

### VI. The Aftermath—And the Questions  
The Flaming Fist arrived eventually. I gave a clipped report, omitting mention of Voras’s heritage (some truths only incite panic). They dismissed it as a botched robbery. The scribe, wisely, kept silent. 

I left Baldur’s Gate at dawn, but I’ve turned the encounter over in my mind for years. Voras wasn’t evil incarnate; he was sharp edges forged by cruelty. A soul torn between realms, weaponizing his pain. I’ve known humans—champions of supposedly noble causes—who’ve done far worse with far less reason. 

These days, when I advise Silverymoon’s gemcutters’ guild on "security concerns" (guild politics—don’t ask), I think of Voras. Thieving apprentices, rival guild sabotage—petty grievances. But I’ve seen how bitterness, left to fester, can birth sharper claws than any Abyssal legacy. 

The scribe survived. The cultists? Unclear. Rumors trickled north months later about a burned warehouse in the Outer City and thirteen bodies found with their hearts missing. No shadows clung to the corpses. No star-black blood stained the ashes. 

But sometimes, when the rain pounds my attic window here in Silverymoon, I imagine I see crimson eyes gleaming in the storm-lashed dark. And I wonder—did Voras find what he sought? 

Or is he still hunting, somewhere, his shadow growing longer with every pact made in desperation, every soul sold to fill the void where belonging should live?
