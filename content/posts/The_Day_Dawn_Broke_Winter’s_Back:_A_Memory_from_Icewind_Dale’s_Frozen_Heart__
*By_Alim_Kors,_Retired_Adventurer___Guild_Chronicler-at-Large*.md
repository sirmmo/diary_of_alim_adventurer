Silverymoon’s libraries are full of tales about Icewind Dale—epics of ten towns clinging to life, of frost giants stomping across tundras, and the ever-present breath of Auril’s winter. But the story I carry isn’t about conquest or survival. It’s about witnessing a moment where faith and nature collided like two crashing glaciers, revealing something… radiant.  

It happened thirteen winters past. I’d been hired by the Fellowship of the Frozen Scrolls—a bookish lot in Bryn Shander desperate for an "experienced witness" (read: someone fool enough to trek into the Spine of the World during the Thaw’s false promises). Their scholars suspected an ancient Netherese relic lay buried near the Kelvin’s Cairn glacier, and wanted confirmation before funding a full expedition. I’d seen enough magic to know relics bite back, but coin was short, and Icewind Dale had been kinder to me than most. Or so I’d thought.  

The cold there isn’t just weather—it’s a predator. By day three, even my enchanted gloves felt thin. My guide, a taciturn dwarven scout named Thordak, muttered about "sky-sickness" as the northern lights began writhing earlier than usual, their greens and violets churning like a disturbed sea. That’s when we stumbled onto *her*.  

Gwyneira Frostmantle—a paladin of Lathander, she declared, though her armor bore no house sigils, only dents and the faded gold of dawn-prayer engravings. She’d been tracking a white moose (“A sign from the Morninglord,” she insisted) that led her into the path of yeti hunters. They’d scattered when the earth groaned. Now she stood knee-deep in fresh snow, staring at the warping auroras with the focus of a hawk.  

“The Light Eternal stirs beneath the ice,” she said, as though we’d asked. Her voice carried a warmth that shouldn’t have existed that far north. “Can you not feel it?”  

Thordak rolled his eyes. “Feel my toes begging for a fire, aye.”  

But I *did* feel it. A prickle in the air, like before a lightning strike. The snow underfoot hummed.  

---  

We made camp in a shallow ice cave. Gwyneira spoke little, polishing her sword—Dawn’s Reprieve—with methodical reverence. She’d taken oaths at a sunrise monastery in Impiltur, she told us, but her path led her “where dawn is hardest to see.” Icewind Dale, it seemed, qualified.  

Around midnight, the cave began to *drip*.  

Not melt—drip. Rivulets snaked down the walls as steam rose from Gwyneira’s pauldrons. Thordak yelped, scrambling from his bedroll as a puddle spread beneath him.  

“What devilry is this?” he growled, axe in hand.  

The paladin rose, her breath no longer fogging. “No devilry. A reckoning.”  

Outside, the northern lights had congealed into a single, throbbing sphere of light—a second moon, but molten gold. It cast long, liquid shadows across the snowfields. And the glacier…  

Kelvin’s Cairn was *moving*.  

Not calving or cracking. *Swaying.* Like seaweed in a tide. The ice sang—a low, harmonic drone that set my teeth buzzing. In thirty years of skirting wizard duels and dragon roars, I’d never heard anything like it.  

Gwyneira strode into the open, Dawn’s Reprieve raised high. “The Sleeping Light awakens!” she cried—not in Common, but in a tongue that tasted of honey and hearth-smoke. Celestial, I realized. Liturgy.  

Thordak gripped my arm. “We should flee. Now.”  

But the scholar in me—the part that once traded half a library to a green dragon for safe passage—held fast. “Wait. *Watch.*”  

---  

What followed defied every natural law I know.  

The glacier *bent*. Not fractured, not collapsed, but *curved* toward the golden sphere, ice flowing like syrup. Gwyneira’s blade blazed, its light fusing with the sphere into a beam that struck the glacier’s heart. And there, in the dead of an Icewind Dale night, winter cracked open.  

A geyser erupted—not of water, but pure, radiant warmth. It tore through the glacier’s crest, billowing upward in a plume of vapor and prismatic mist. Within that column, shapes flickered: winged seraphs of molten light, spiraling upwards. The snow for a mile around didn’t just melt—it *bloomed*. Frostflowers erupted, their crystalline petals unfolding like parchment in fire, glowing from within.  

Gwyneira stood at the epicenter, armor incandescent. Her prayer wasn’t words now—it was resonance. The earth *answered*.  

Thordak fell to his knees, swearing in Dwarvish. I just laughed. Laughed like a madman, tears freezing on my cheeks. It wasn’t joy. It was the helpless giddiness of seeing scales fall from your eyes. Here was a truth I’d forgotten in decades of negotiating guild tariffs and parsing ancient traps: the world is *alive*. And sometimes, it chooses to roar.  

The light-show lasted perhaps ten minutes. When it faded, the glacier stood whole again—but transformed. Its face now bore a carving no mortal chisel could etch: a towering sunburst, fifty feet wide, its rays deep as canyon clefts. Meltwater pooled at its base, steaming gently, and frostflowers glittered around it like a shattered crown.  

Gwyneira knelt, breath ragged, Dawn’s Reprieve dimmed but unbroken. “The Light beneath remembered,” she whispered. Whether she meant the glacier, the Dale, or herself, I couldn’t say.  

---  

The Fellowship never found their Netherese relic. Thordak quit guiding to become a priest of Moradin (“If ice can sing, my beard’s worth listening to”). As for Gwyneira? She vanished by morning, leaving only footprints leading east—toward the sunrise, of course.  

But to this day, Dwarven miners speak of the “Sun-Pocked Glacier,” where ice never reforms over that carved sunburst. Frostflowers bloom year-round there, immune to blight or blizzard. Pilgrims leave dawn-touched coins in the pool, though none agree which god claims them.  

I keep one of those frostflowers on my Silverymoon desk—encased in resin, never melting. Guildmasters sniff at my “storybook theatrics,” but on winter mornings when dawn spills through my window, that flower glows faintly gold. A reminder that even in the hungriest cold, light hibernates. Waiting.  

Perhaps that’s why I bother with guild politics—not for the gold, but the chance to nudge some weary soul toward *their* dawn. Gwyneira would approve. Lathander knows I do.
