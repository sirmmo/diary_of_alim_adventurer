It’s a curious thing, retirement. In Silverymoon’s ivory libraries or guild halls perfumed with pipe-smoke and ambition, my old bones find comfort in tales spun for rapt audiences. But time has sanded the sharper edges from my memories, leaving only the echoes of thunderous moments. One such echo still shakes me awake some nights—the humid, blood-scented air of Baldur’s Gate, and the night an army of trolls tried to chew the city down to its seawall foundations. This was no ordinary raid. It was a force of nature, a grotesque ballet under a cursed moon. Let me tell you of the Trollclaw Moon, and the night the very stones of the Gate trembled.  

---

### **I. The Calm Before the Green Storm**  
Baldur’s Gate in the month of Eleasis is a city drunk on its own sweat. The salt-stink of the Sea of Swords mingles with the reek of packed bodies in the Lower City markets, where hawkers peddle everything from Amnian silks to fried griffon eggs. I’d docked there in 1479 DR, chasing whispers of a relic lost beneath the Basilisk Gate—a fool’s errand, as it turned out. But the gods laugh while weaving fortunes; my failure led me to witness something far stranger.  

That year, Eleasis brought more than heat. Rumors slithered through taverns like serpents: goblin cartels vanishing from the Outer City, fishermen’s nets hauling up gnawed hippocampi bones, and a greasy green algae blooming in the harbor. Dismissed as summer superstitions, they were the first stitches in a tapestry of dread.  

The Trollclaw Moon rose on the 19th—a swollen, jaundiced orb streaked with volcanic ash from the distant Cloak Wood eruptions. The Gate celebrated nonetheless. In the Wide, merchants threw the annual "Tymora’s Tide" festival, where drunk nobles tossed gold coins into the river for luck. I’d taken a room at the Blushing Mermaid, nursing a flagon of Elturian red, when the first scream cut through the lute music.  

---

### **II. The Tide Turns Foul**  
They came from the river.  

Not the handful of cave trolls the Flaming Fist occasionally culled from the Trollclaws, but a *host*. Fifty? A hundred? Torchlight glistened off hunched, gangrene-green backs as they clawed from the Chionthar’s murk, their stench—a nose-searing cocktail of wet fur, necrotic flesh, and bile—rolling ahead like fog. These weren’t starving wanderers. They moved with eerie coordination, shambling yet deliberate, herded by some primal instinct. Or worse, *driven*.  

Chaos erupted. Revelers stampeded, crushing stalls into splinters. A troll the size of an owlbear vaulted onto the Wide’s central fountain, shredding a bard mid-ballad. I remember the crimson spray hitting a carved marble statue of Balduran, turning the explorer’s stone eyes into ghastly weeping sockets.  

I fought, of course. Retirement hadn’t dulled my blade nor my oath to shield the helpless. But this was no skirmish. It was a storm of teeth and claws, a grotesque mirror of the crashing waves beyond the seawall. Trolls regenerated faster than our blades could cut—sever a limb, and the damn thing *sprouted* a new one, raw and glistening like undercooked meat. Fire was scarce; the festival’s lanterns were decorative, not tactical. We fell back, bleeding, to the sanctum of the High Hall steps.  

---

### **III. The Dance of Fire and Flesh**  
Here’s what most tales omit: Baldur’s Gate’s true savior that night wasn’t steel or spell. It was bureaucracy.  

Captain Varl of the Flaming Fist—a man whose beard smelled perpetually of cheap gin—had gambled the city’s safety on a contingency even his superiors mocked: the "Wyrm’s Pyre Protocol." Stored in Warehouses E-12 and E-13 near the docks were sixty barrels of alchemist’s fire, stockpiled for "extreme municipal discouragement."  

We formed a ragged line along the Elevated Plaza, crossbowmen perched on rooftops plinking futilely at regenerating skulls. I stood beside a half-elf alchemist named Thessa, her hands trembling as she lit pitch-soaked arrows.  

"Ever seen a troll horde broken?" she shouted over the din.  

"Not without a dragon!" I yelled back.  

The first barrel, lobbed by a trebuchet crewed by panicked dockworkers, arced over the carnage like a tiny comet. It struck the cobbles just as the lead troll—a hulking brute with a deer skull lashed to its head—reached the base of the stairs. The explosion painted the night in searing white. Trolls shrieked as unnatural metabolism turned their own healing against them; flaming flesh regrew only to burn anew.  

But trolls are cunning. They *learned*. Some began hurling debris—carts, corpses, chunks of masonry—to detonate barrels mid-air. Others clambered up buildings, peeling off roofs to crush the archers below. A gnoll mercenary beside me took a flung stone the size of a wine cask to the chest. His last words were, "Tell my wives… I lied about the loot—" before dissolving into red mist.  

---

### **IV. Eclipse and Revelation**  
Near midnight, as the battle turned into a war of attrition, the moon’s sickness revealed itself fully. The Trollclaw Moon’s ash veil parted, and we saw it: a lunar eclipse, but inverted. Instead of shadow swallowing light, a *green* radiance pulsed from the moon’s heart, bathing the city in emerald twilight.  

Under that glow, the trolls… changed.  

Their regeneration slowed. Their milky eyes cleared, revealing not mindless hunger, but *terror*. They began retreating, clawing over each other to flee back into the Chionthar. One, pinned under a collapsed wall, met my gaze. In its eyes, I saw recognition—and despair. Then the alchemist’s fire took it.  

Later, scholars would debate the eclipse. Was it Syranita the Green Dragon, archmage of the Gate, testing some new magic? A spillover from the elemental chaos? Or the land itself rebelling against the city’s hubris? The trolls, they claimed, were mere pawns—drawn by the corrupted moon’s call like salmon to spawning grounds, their regenerative fury amplified until the eclipse severed the tether.  

I have a simpler theory: the world breathes. Sometimes, it coughs.  

---

### **V. Aftermath: Bones and Questions**  
By dawn, the Lower City resembled a butcher’s fever dream. Green-tinged smoke coiled from still-smoldering troll carcasses. The Flaming Fist lost sixty-three souls; civilians numbered over two hundred dead. The relic I’d sought? Found, ironically, in the rubble of a troll’s gullet—a silver locket engraved with a Harper sigil, half-digested but intact.  

But the true cost was subtler. For months, the Gate’s children drew pictures of "moon trolls" in school tablets. The algae in the harbor thickened into floating islands, choking off trade. And in the Outer City, where the refugee tents huddled, preachers shouted of divine wrath.  

Years later, I stood at the reforged Wyrm’s Crossing, watching sewage and rainwater bleed into the Chionthar. A young guardsman asked me, "Is it true trolls can’t drown?"  

I thought of the glowing moon, the desperate retreat. "Everything drowns, lad," I said. "Even nightmares."  

---

### **Reflections from a Silverymoon Balcony**  
Now, sipping summer wine as the River Rauvin glitters below, I see that night for what it was: a reminder. We build walls, forge treaties, and light bonfires to pretend we’ve tamed the wilds. But beneath every city’s cobblestones, the old world stirs. Trolls, after all, are just one flavor of chaos.  

I keep the melted deer skull from the lead troll’s brow on my mantel. Not a trophy, but a tutor. It whispers: *You are small. The world is vast. Stay humble.*  

And so I do. Though if the guildmasters of Silverymoon start complaining about "goblin vermin" in the sewers again, I might just gift them that skull. Some lessons bear repeating.  

\- Alim Kors, once of the Blushing Mermaid, now of the River’s Song Guildhouse, Silverymoon.
