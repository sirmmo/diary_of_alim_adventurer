The jungles of Chult have a way of carving themselves into your soul. You don’t forget the humidity that clings like a second skin, the chorus of howler monkeys that becomes your lullaby, or the way ancient stones whisper secrets beneath vines thicker than a troll’s wrist. But of all the terrors and wonders I witnessed in that green hell, one night stands apart—a night when the veil between worlds frayed, and a man named John showed me that courage and faith can bend even the laws of the gods.  

I’ll set the scene for you: It was the wet season of 1490 DR, and my team—a handful of battered sellswords and desperate scholars—had been trekking for weeks in search of a fabled “City of Whispering Shadows” glimpsed on a tattered map. Rations were low, tempers high. John, a quiet, broad-shouldered Watcher of Helm from the Sword Coast, had joined us in Port Nyanzaru as a hired blade. He wasn’t the talkative type, but he had a way of steadying the group with nothing but a nod. We’d survived yuan-ti ambushes and zombie tyrannosaur packs together, but nothing prepared us for what lurked in that unnamed valley.  

We stumbled upon the ruins at twilight. At first glance, it was just another overgrown Chultan relic—moss-eaten ziggurat steps, crumbling obelisks etched with serpents and spirals. But then the air changed. Thunder rolled without clouds. The torches flickered green. Our guide, a seasoned Chultan hunter, whispered, “*Ubtao’s ghosts walk here*,” before vanishing into the trees, leaving us to face the gathering dark alone.  

John sensed it first. He froze mid-step, gauntleted hand raised. “Do you hear that?” Wind hissed through the stones in a melody no breeze should make—a dirge in a language none of us knew, yet *understood*. The ground trembled. Shadows pooled like ink at the ziggurat’s base, coalescing into figures—tall, shimmering beings with starlight for eyes and crowns of antlers and smoke. They weren’t undead. They were something older. Something that made my amulet of Lathander burn cold against my chest.  

“Godsbelow,” breathed Elsior, our elven archer, nocking an arrow. “Guardian spirits?”  

“No,” John said, his voice eerily calm. “Echoes.”  

That’s when the visions began.  

The ruins vanished. We stood in a thriving city—golden towers, feathered priests chanting to a sky choked with winged serpents. A god-king with eyes like molten copper raised his hands, and the horizon *ripped*. Portals yawned open, vomiting forth creatures of teeth and tendrils. The city fell in screams. The god-king was torn apart by his own magic. Then—silence. The jungle returned, but the phantom figures still surrounded us, weeping silver tears.  

One drifted toward John. Its hand stretched out, and where it pointed, the air shimmered like a mirage. A tear in reality, no wider than a dagger, hovered beside the ziggurat. Through it, I saw *us*—minutes earlier, standing in the same spot, debating whether to camp. The spirit was showing us the moment the rift had been born. Dread coiled in my gut. If time itself was fraying here…  

“It wants us to mend it,” John said. Simple. Certain. As if fixing a crack in the cosmos was a day’s work.  

“Mend it *how*?” Elsior snapped. “We’re not archmages!”  

The spirits moaned. The rift pulsed, widening. From its edges, tendrils of distorted time lashed out. One grazed our mage, Vaelis, aging his left arm to bone in seconds before he severed it with a scream. Chaos erupted. Elsior’s arrows passed through the spirits like mist. My own blade fared no better. Only John stood firm, muttering prayers to Helm, his shield raised.  

Then—it happened. The spirits turned to him in unison, their starlight eyes sharpening. The fiercest one drifted forward and laid a translucent hand on John’s chest. His armor didn’t repel it. It sank *into* him, and John gasped, back arching as light seared from his eyes and mouth. For a heartbeat, he burned like a fallen star.  

We thought him dead.  

But when the light faded, John still stood, breathing hard, changed. His voice boomed with a hundred layered whispers: “**The Weaver’s thread is cut. The Labyrinth bleeds. Seal the wound.**” He moved with purpose to the rift, Helm’s symbol glowing like forge-fire on his breastplate. The spirits followed, circling him like mourners.  

John thrust his gauntlet into the rift. Reality screamed. The ground split. I watched in awe as the tear stitched itself closed—not with spells, but with something deeper. Faith? Sacrifice? When it was done, John collapsed, the light in him extinguished. The spirits faded, their weeping now a sigh of relief.  

We carried John out at dawn. He remembered nothing but a “dream of falling through stars.” The ruins? Gone. The valley? Just jungle again. No traces of the rift, the spirits—or Vaelis’s arm.  

I’ve replayed that night a thousand times. Was it Ubtao’s lost children pleading for peace? A time-wound from the days of the Ebon Triad? John insists Helm granted him no divine insight. Perhaps it doesn’t matter. What stays with me is the look on his face as he faced the rift—not courage, but compassion. He didn’t see monsters. He saw suffering.  

These days, when guildmasters in Silverymoon scoff at reports of “anomalies” in the High Forest or grumble about funding expeditions to clean up “old magic,” I tell them John’s story. The past never truly dies in Faerûn. It waits, in stones and shadows, for someone willing to listen.  

And the next tear in the world’s fabric? May Helm grant us another John when it comes.
